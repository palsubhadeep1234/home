<p class="lede">Transferring molecular mechanisms into efficient models that can explain and predict larger-scale behavior.</p>

## The scientific question

How can we simplify a molecular system while preserving the physics that matters for a particular engineering problem? My work uses material- and property-specific mappings, tested through molecular reference calculations and experimental collaboration.

## Research foundations

### Implicit chain particle modeling

I co-developed the implicit chain particle model (ICPM), which represents a polymer-grafted nanoparticle as an effective particle. Interactions informed by molecular strain-energy density enable larger-scale calculations while retaining important features of stress–strain response, modulus, and toughness.

[ICPM · Macromolecules, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.macromol.2c02272)

### Polymer conformation and nanocomposite mechanics

I used ICPM simulations to interpret experimentally observed changes in nanoparticle-composite stiffness. The modeling connected activated polymer volume and interparticle cohesion to the mechanical response of confined polymers.

[Polymer-conformation programming · Advanced Materials, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202415352)

### Cement paste rheology

I transferred atomistically calculated, water-mediated interactions between tricalcium-silicate surfaces to particle-scale models of fresh cement paste. The simulations reproduced shear-thinning trends and revealed contact breakage and reformation under shear, connecting molecular interactions to flow relevant to additive manufacturing.

[Cement paste rheology · Journal of Applied Physics, 2026 · Editor’s Pick](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0332157)

### Single-chain deformation and continuum mechanics

I developed finite-element indentation models to interpret super-resolution observations of polymer-chain deformation. Mapping the simulated displacement and strain fields to chain orientation connected continuum loading to experimentally observed molecular alignment.

[Single-chain imaging · Physical Review Letters, 2025 · Editor’s Suggestion](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.148101)
