This category covers projects where the primary methodological contribution is bridging vastly different length or time scales — from nanoscale molecular interactions to microscale continuum behavior — using finite element analysis, potential-of-mean-force calculations, or strain-field mapping. Both projects involve close collaboration with experimental groups, with simulations directly validating or interpreting experimental observations.

### 11. Cement Paste Rheology for 3D-Printing Applications
*Advisors/Collaborators: Dr. Sinan Keten; Dr. Faramarz Joodaki; Dr. Gianluca Cusatis* · **2023 – 2025**

- Created a **nanoscale** C3S–water model and computed the **potential of mean force (PMF)** between cement surfaces across varying water contents to inform a micron-scale rheology model.
- Simulated **nozzle flow** in 3D printing and found viscosity decreases with increasing shear rate and water-to-cement ratio, matching rheological expectations for printable cement pastes.

**Related publication:**
- **Pal, S.**, Wu, Z., Joodaki, F., Cusatis, G., & Keten, S., *Molecular Investigation of Freshly-made Cement Paste Rheology for 3D-printing Application.* **Journal of Physical Chemistry** *(In review)*

### 12. Real-Time Super-Resolution Imaging of Polymer-Chain Conformational Changes
*Advisors/Collaborators: Dr. Sinan Keten; Ruiqi Xiao; Dr. Muzhou Wang* · **2023 – 2024**

- Built **finite-element models** of bottlebrush-polymer thin films with rigid indentation and frictional contact to mirror experiments (scaling to ~**10⁷** DOF in **ABAQUS**).
- Quantified **single-chain orientation changes** by mapping vector fields through the simulated **strain field**; simulations validated experimental observations, giving molecular-level insight into chain motion — an innovative technique in the polymer physics field.

**Related publication:**
- Xiao, R., **Pal, S.**, Rademacher, C., Chen, J., Wang, Q., Chen, W., Keten, S., Shull, K. R., & Wang, M., *Real-Time Visualization of Polymer Single Chain Conformational Change in Bulk State Using Super-Resolution Microscopy.* **Physical Review Letters**, 2025. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.148101](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.148101) [*Editor's Suggestion*]
