<p class="lede">AI-guided materials design through multiscale fracture mechanics.</p>
<p>My long-term goal is to connect molecular networks, interfaces, and defects to reliable mechanical performance. Building on my <a href="{{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}">current research and established capabilities</a>, I plan to combine mechanistic understanding, efficient predictive models, and human-supervised hypothesis testing.</p>
<p>The three proposed thrusts below are mutually reinforcing. Molecular simulations and collaborative experiments will establish and test the physics; reduced models and AI will accelerate design exploration.</p>

<section class="section research-plan" id="impact">
<div class="research-feature"><div><span class="label">Thrust 01</span><h2>Defect engineering for lightweight impact protection</h2><p class="research-question">How can defects improve impact protection while maintaining strength and low weight?</p><p>Voids, weak interfaces, and structural heterogeneity can trigger fracture, but they can also promote distributed deformation. I aim to determine when imperfections become useful design variables and when they cause premature failure.</p></div><figure><img src="{{ '/images/research/defect-engineering.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Proposed multiscale defect simulations and surrogate models guiding impact-protection design" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Proposed workflow: connect defect geometry and interactions to mechanical response, then optimize protective architectures.</figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="plan-body"><h3>Proposed approach</h3><ol><li><strong>Map defects to properties.</strong> Use molecular simulations to vary void geometry, distribution, and particle proximity, comparing dispersed and clustered defects at matched void volume fraction. Local stress, chain rearrangements, and cavity evolution will identify the mechanisms.</li><li><strong>Predict fracture and impact across scales.</strong> Transfer molecular response to particle models and rate-dependent constitutive and fracture descriptions for finite-element models of layered protective structures. Independent fracture and impact tests will assess predictions.</li><li><strong>Optimize under realistic constraints.</strong> Combine multi-fidelity surrogates and Bayesian optimization to balance strength, penetration resistance, and energy absorption under mass and transmitted-force constraints. Evaluate both predictive uncertainty and material variability.</li></ol>
<h3>Intended outcomes</h3><p>Quantitative defect–property maps, experimentally assessed multiscale models, and allowable defect ranges for lightweight protective architectures. Longer-term applications include wearable protection, vehicle panels, and protective enclosures for energy-storage systems.</p></div>
<div class="research-connections"><span>Builds on:</span><a href="{{ '/projects/pgn/' | relative_url }}">PGN impact mechanics & active learning</a><a href="{{ '/projects/multiscale/' | relative_url }}">Physics-preserving scale transfer</a></div>
</section>
<section class="section research-plan" id="networks">
<div class="research-feature"><div><span class="label">Thrust 02</span><h2>Accelerate thermoset design through reduced network dynamics</h2><p class="research-question">Can reduced network dynamics predict epoxy mechanics and identify realizable formulations with targeted properties?</p><p>Chemistry and curing determine molecular connectivity, but conventional descriptors do not fully explain deformation and failure. I aim to develop physically interpretable network representations that enable rapid prediction and inverse design.</p></div><figure><img src="{{ '/images/research/network-dynamics.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Proposed reduction of molecular networks into graph coordinates and learned mechanical dynamics" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Proposed methodology: reduce evolving network descriptions while retaining the physics needed to predict mechanical response.</figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="plan-body"><h3>Proposed approach</h3><ol><li><strong>Connect architecture to mechanics.</strong> Represent crosslinks and molecular strands as graphs carrying chemical identity, length, and orientation. Compare network realizations at matched composition and conversion to isolate the roles of loops, dangling strands, and connectivity heterogeneity.</li><li><strong>Learn reduced dynamics.</strong> Use diffusion maps to identify a small set of coordinates and neural networks to learn their evolution under loading. Connect formulation and curing to initial network states so that design searches yield realizable candidates.</li><li><strong>Assess predictions independently.</strong> Test formulations and loading histories excluded from training using molecular simulations and collaborative tensile, cyclic-loading, and stress-relaxation experiments. Compare performance with conventional descriptors.</li></ol>
<h3>Intended outcomes</h3><p>Relationships linking formulation, curing, network structure, and performance; and a reduced-order simulator for rapid screening and inverse design within an experimentally assessed range. Longer-term extensions include dynamic-bond thermosets, elastomers, gels, and stress-relaxing adhesives.</p></div>
<div class="research-connections"><span>Builds on:</span><a href="{{ '/projects/crosslinked-networks/' | relative_url }}">Epoxy fracture & network topology</a><a href="{{ '/projects/#experiments' | relative_url }}">Experimental model assessment</a></div>
</section>
<section class="section research-plan" id="ai">
<div class="research-feature"><div><span class="label">Thrust 03</span><h2>Enable materials discovery with traceable AI</h2><p class="research-question">Can adaptive, skill-guided AI turn scientific questions into reliable investigations and evidence-supported recommendations?</p><p>I aim to develop a human-supervised framework that connects evidence-linked hypotheses, adaptive agent coordination, and a tested library of scientific procedures. Every recommendation should retain its sources, assumptions, uncertainty, and unresolved questions.</p></div><figure><img src="{{ '/images/research/traceable-ai.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Proposed human-supervised system connecting competing hypotheses, planning, research agents, and synthesis" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Proposed framework: traceable planning, adaptive investigation, and evidence synthesis with human oversight.</figcaption></figure></div>
<div class="plan-body"><h3>Proposed approach</h3><ol><li><strong>Generate evidence-linked hypotheses.</strong> Connect claims to source passages, assumptions, supporting and opposing findings, and calibrated confidence. Use unresolved conflicts to propose competing mechanisms and discriminating tests.</li><li><strong>Coordinate adaptive investigations.</strong> Combine planning and synthesis agents with research agents that use literature databases, knowledge graphs, physics models, and an editable skill library. Test and review new procedures before reuse, while enforcing physical constraints and model-applicability limits.</li><li><strong>Evaluate reliability and reproducibility.</strong> Preserve hypothesis revisions and computational records. Compare with single-agent and fixed-team workflows using accuracy, source fidelity, confidence calibration, reproducibility, and computational cost.</li></ol>
<h3>Intended outcomes</h3><p>An auditable hypothesis graph and a human-supervised platform for reproducible materials investigation. Initial applications will draw on defect-tolerant composites and polymer networks from the first two thrusts.</p></div>
