<section class="section" id="ai">
<p class="eyebrow">Ongoing work / MIT</p><h2>Traceable reasoning and collective scientific discovery</h2>
<p>I investigate how scientific AI can connect hypotheses to evidence and coordinated computational tests. In <strong>Graph-PRefLexOR</strong>, I co-developed graph-native reasoning, designed a 100-question materials-science benchmark, and analyzed reasoning representations. This work makes the steps between mechanism exploration and hypothesis synthesis explicit.</p>
<p>Through <strong>ScienceClaw + Infinite</strong> and <strong>SwarmWorld</strong>, I study how agents use shared artifacts, computational skills, and environmental interactions to conduct collective investigations. My contributions include a polymer-physics skill module, an elastomer-design workflow, and computational experiments on emergent specialization.</p>
<p>In collaboration with Saint-Gobain, I also helped develop hierarchical reasoning for evidence-grounded material substitution, proposing a PFAS-substitute multilayer and a laboratory-scale processing route. Ongoing work on cementitious systems connects knowledge graphs and scientific agents to questions of rheology and printability.</p>

<section class="section" id="mechanisms">
<p class="eyebrow">Research foundations / Polymer mechanics</p><h2>Molecular mechanisms of deformation and fracture</h2>
<p>My doctoral work established how <strong>cohesion, polymer interpenetration, entanglements, and particle packing</strong> govern the impact response and stiffness–toughness balance of polymer-grafted nanoparticle assemblies. Molecular simulations revealed how architecture changes failure mechanisms, while Gaussian-process surrogates and active learning guided the search for improved binary assemblies.</p>
<p>In epoxy networks, I connected hardener chemistry and molecular failure to continuum fracture-energy estimates. Complementary studies of adaptable networks and bio-inspired catch bonds investigated how connectivity and load sharing control reprocessability and force-dependent adhesion.</p>

<section class="section" id="multiscale">
<p class="eyebrow">Research foundations / Physics-preserving models</p><h2>Transferring molecular physics across scales</h2>
<p>I develop reduced descriptions that retain the physical mechanisms relevant to a particular material and loading condition. The <strong>implicit chain particle model (ICPM)</strong>, which I co-developed, maps each polymer-grafted nanoparticle to an effective particle using molecular strain-energy information. It enables larger-scale mechanics calculations while retaining important features of stress–strain response, modulus, and toughness.</p>
<p>Other mappings connect molecular epoxy quantities to fracture energy and water-mediated cement-surface interactions to particle-scale rheology. These studies demonstrate how distinct molecular mechanisms can inform efficient engineering-scale models.</p>

<section class="section" id="experiments">
<p class="eyebrow">Research foundations / Computational–experimental collaboration</p><h2>Testing models against material behavior</h2>
<p>Across collaborations, I use simulations to interpret experiments and explore conditions beyond those directly measured. ICPM calculations connected polymer confinement and interparticle cohesion to nanocomposite stiffness. Molecular relaxation studies linked processing history to interpenetration, entanglements, and toughness.</p>
<p>In covalent adaptable networks, virtual bond cleavage and cluster analysis tested a percolation boundary relevant to reprocessability. Finite-element indentation models also connected continuum deformation to polymer-chain alignment observed through super-resolution microscopy.</p>
