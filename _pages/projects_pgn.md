## The scientific question

Why do some polymer nanocomposites dissipate impact energy effectively while others fail prematurely? My work connects polymer cohesion, interpenetration, entanglements, and nanoparticle packing to mechanical performance.

## Research foundations

### Molecular mechanisms of impact resistance

During my PhD, I used coarse-grained molecular simulations of polymer-grafted nanoparticle (PGN) films to investigate micro-ballistic response. The work identified cohesive-energy density as a governing descriptor and showed how graft architecture changes failure mechanisms and energy dissipation. Intermediate graft lengths balance penetration resistance and projectile deceleration.

[Micro-ballistic response · Soft Matter, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4SM00718B)

### Active learning for competing properties

I combined molecular simulations, Gaussian-process surrogate models, and Bayesian optimization to explore binary PGN architectures. This work connects packing and load transfer to the stiffness–toughness balance and uses informative simulations to search a large design space efficiently.

[Binary PGNs and active learning · Macromolecules, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.macromol.6c00682)

### Processing, confinement, and shear

In collaborative studies, I investigated how nanoparticle reinforcement, polymer interdigitation, and load-bearing entanglements govern shear response and toughness. Molecular relaxation simulations helped explain why processing history changes the mechanical properties of PGN films.

- [Shear mechanics · Journal of Chemical Physics, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0188494)
- [Nanoconfinement release · Nano Letters, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c00680)
- [Process-driven properties · ACS Nano, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.6c00412)
